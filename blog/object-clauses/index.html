Object clauses (objective clause)

When an object clause introduced by that serves as the object of a verb or certain adjectives, that can be omitted in spoken and informal English, and it often is.

Cases where that cannot be omitted

  1. When object clauses introduced by that serve as the object of a verb and the verb is followed by several parallel that-clauses, only the first that, right next to the verb, can be omitted; none of the others can. For example:

  2. When a that object clause is placed at the start of the sentence for emphasis, that cannot be omitted. For example:

    • That she is a good girl I know. I know that she is a good girl.

    • That he ever said such a thing I simply don’t believe. I simply don't believe he ever said such a thing.

  3. When the that-clause is separated from the verb by other words or clauses, that should generally not be omitted, to avoid confusing the meaning. For example:

    • They told us once again that the situation was serious. They told us once more that the situation was serious.

    • The dealer told me how much he was prepared to pay for my car and that I could have the money without delay. The dealer told me what he would pay for my car and said I would receive the money right away.
