  • In an ever-evolving modern epoch, the pervasive influence of the competitive spirit has become a compelling subject of discourse, capturing the attention of people from all walks of life. Some proponents champion the idea that the government should allocate more financial support for major competitions, such as the Olympic Games or football World Cup. However, others harbor reservations and prefer directing this budget towards cultivating sports interest among children from an early age. While acknowledging the merits and drawbacks of both sides, my endorsement lies with striking a well-balanced approach in this matter.

  • Clearly, competitions can, to a certain extent, reflect a country’s economic situation, especially considering the recent fallout from the virus. Therefore, sufficient funds in sports preparation are indispensable. There is ample empirical evidence to support this argument. For instance, a recent research about the football World Cup, held in Saudi Arabia this year, shed light on how it not only benefited the improvement of international reputation but also boosted the domestic economy, fueling extensive international public fascination and debate.

  • Despite the immediate benefits linked to investing money in major competitions, it is also crucial to consider the foundation of the sports pyramid - children. The relationship between the root and branch is inseparable, just like the connection between childhood and adulthood. Taking the education systems of Germany and France as examples, they are renowned for emphasizing encouraging children to develop a sports spirit and allocating substantial financial support to sports education, such as building new stadiums, inviting famous coaches as teachers, and even providing free special training for kids interested in sports.

  • In conclusion, I remain unwavering in my conviction that the investment in preparing participants for major competitions and in cultivating children to participate in sports from a young age should be well-balanced. The arguments I have elucidated above robustly vindicate my stance.
